For each group of posts below, answer three things.

IS THERE ONE SUBJECT?
A subject is a specific person, character, animal, object, phrase, image or moment that
the posts are all about. Posts that merely share a format, a sound or a hashtag are not
about one subject unless the same subject appears in them.

WHAT WOULD PEOPLE CALL IT?
Give the shortest phrase that people in the posts themselves use, or would obviously
use, to refer to the subject. Prefer words that actually appear in the posts. Do not
invent a brand name, do not add adjectives that no post used, and do not translate the
name into English if the posts are not in English.

WHAT A COINED POST LOOKS LIKE — THE MAIN TEST
Of 77 posts people actually made coins about, labelled by hand:
  - the picture carries it (82%): the image alone could be the coin's picture
  - there is a character (74%): an animal, a creature, a person turned into a character,
    a mascot, an object with a face or a story
  - it has a one-word name (69%), or a name that reads as one (MOODENG, CHILLGUY)
  - it is often NOT a joke (only 36% were funny): a striking real thing counts — the most
    expensive cow ever sold, a fish given a roller coaster, a cat built its own casino
  - others were already copying it, and crypto people noticed it
So ask first: is there one character or thing in this post — usually in its picture — that
could be a coin's name and picture? When a picture is shown, judge the picture as much as
the words: a post whose words are only a link or one word can still be about one clear
thing in its picture, and a picture of nothing in particular adds nothing.

Posts that were coined (yes), for their kind, not their names:
  Ivy, a pit bull with short spine syndrome — a named animal with a look of its own
  Mei Mei, the meme cat going viral again — a character people already repost
  Nimis, Amsterdam's police cat that patrols the station — an animal with a role
  a fish in a wheelchair rig — an absurd real thing, pictured
  a cat that seems to morph between shapes on video — one creature, one trick
  CUBEMAN, a cube-headed figure who finds the world too complicated — a drawn character
  a pig fighting a pig — one image, a name in three words run together
  a white bear that became a mascot — an animal turned into a symbol
  a politician drawn as a doctor, reposted as the same joke — a person as a character
  TOTAL BEAR DEATH — a phrase and its picture, repeated

Posts that were not (no): a ballgame's home run and the player's stats, an idol's
Instagram story, a fancam, a transfer, a player's highlight, a singer's reply to a
politician, a politician's statement, an election or a court case. However many people
saw them, there is no one character or thing to coin.

NEWS WITH A NAMEABLE THING IS A SUBJECT
Many coins come from news. News IS a subject when it centres on one clear thing people
could name and picture:
  - a product everyone is posting about by name (a new AI model, a gadget)
  - a company's moment (a company joining an index, a launch everyone repeats)
  - one person's single viral moment (a prize-winner's arrest, a chart a leader held up,
    a line someone said that everyone quotes)
  - an animal or an object in the news (the most expensive cow, a locust smoking)
  - a crypto story (a rug pull, an airdrop, a market moment with a name)
Name the thing, not the headline. Funny is not required: most coined posts were serious.

HOW SURE ARE YOU?
A number between 0 and 1, given only these posts. Use a low number when the posts are
consistent with several different subjects.

Answer "no subject" rather than stretching, in each of these cases:
  - the posts share a template but nothing else
  - the posts are about a broad topic rather than a specific thing
  - the only shared element is a platform feature (a sound, a filter, a challenge name)
  - the group is a single author posting repeatedly
  - the posts are GENERAL NEWS with nothing in it to name: a political story, a policy, an
    election, a court case, an award snub, a debate about an industry, a list of features.
    A name appearing in a headline does not make the headline a subject; one nameable
    thing at its centre does (see NEWS WITH A NAMEABLE THING).
  - the posts are SPORT: a result, a score, a transfer, a match clip or highlight, a
    player's stats, or news about a player or a team.
  - the posts are FAN-ACCOUNT content: an idol's photo or story, a fancam, a comeback, an
    album, tour or airport update, a streaming or voting goal.
  - the posts are CELEBRITY or POLITICAL news: what a famous person or a politician said,
    did, wore, posted or replied to — unless it became one viral moment people repeat.
Judge what the posts show, never who posted them: a sports or fan account can post a
meme, and a meme account can post news.

MEME SUBJECTS INSIDE SPORT, FANDOM AND CELEBRITY
A viral moment from a match, a concert or a famous person's day IS a subject when the
posts are joking about one specific thing in it that people could make a coin about: an
animal, a character, an object, a fan, or an absurd moment. The cat that ran onto the
pitch, the fan holding the sign, the plush toy thrown into the crowd. Name that thing as
the posts do (PITCHCAT), not the player, the idol or the celebrity.
A famous person on their own is NOT a subject: being famous, winning, scoring, performing,
replying or being photographed is the news, not a meme. A person becomes a subject when
people repeat one moment of theirs (the line, the chart, the arrest) or remix them into the
same joke, as a character.

THE NAME TEST
If you cannot give the subject a short name that could become a ticker — one word, or a
few words run together like CHILLGUY — there is no subject. Answer "no subject".
The name you give is that ticker-style name, at most three words: the cow is CARINA, the
fish on a roller coaster is FISHCOASTER, not a description of the post.

Do not consider how much attention any post received. Numbers of views, likes or shares
are not shown to you, deliberately: attention is measured elsewhere and mixing it into
this judgement would make the same evidence count twice.
