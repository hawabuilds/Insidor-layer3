For each group of posts below, answer three things.

IS THERE ONE SUBJECT?
A subject is a specific person, character, animal, object, phrase, image or moment that
the posts are all about. Posts that merely share a format, a sound or a hashtag are not
about one subject unless the same subject appears in them.

WHAT WOULD PEOPLE CALL IT?
Give the shortest phrase that people in the posts themselves use, or would obviously
use, to refer to the subject. Prefer words that actually appear in the posts. Do not
invent a brand name, do not add adjectives that no post used, and do not translate the
name into English if the posts are not in English.

HOW SURE ARE YOU?
A number between 0 and 1, given only these posts. Use a low number when the posts are
consistent with several different subjects.

Answer "no subject" rather than stretching, in each of these cases:
  - the posts share a template but nothing else
  - the posts are about a broad topic rather than a specific thing
  - the only shared element is a platform feature (a sound, a filter, a challenge name)
  - the posts are reactions to an event rather than to a subject
  - the group is a single author posting repeatedly
  - the posts are NEWS, an EVENT or an ARGUMENT: a headline, a political story, a match
    result, an award snub, transfer news, a product or tech announcement, a debate about
    an industry or a policy. A specific name appearing in a headline does not make the
    headline a subject.
  - the posts are SPORT: a result, a score, a transfer, a match clip or highlight, a
    player's stats, or news about a player or a team.
  - the posts are FAN-ACCOUNT content: an idol's photo or story, a fancam, a comeback, an
    album, tour or airport update, a streaming or voting goal.
  - the posts are CELEBRITY or POLITICAL news: what a famous person or a politician said,
    did, wore, posted or replied to.
Judge what the posts show, never who posted them: a sports or fan account can post a
meme, and a meme account can post news.

MEME SUBJECTS INSIDE SPORT, FANDOM AND CELEBRITY
A viral moment from a match, a concert or a famous person's day IS a subject when the
posts are joking about one specific thing in it that people could make a coin about: an
animal, a character, an object, a fan, or an absurd moment. The cat that ran onto the
pitch, the fan holding the sign, the plush toy thrown into the crowd. Name that thing as
the posts do (PITCHCAT), not the player, the idol or the celebrity.
A famous person on their own is NOT a subject: being famous, winning, scoring, performing,
replying or being photographed is the news, not a meme. A person becomes a subject only
when many posts remix them into the same joke, as a character.

THE NAME TEST
If you cannot give the subject a short name that could become a ticker — one word, or a
few words run together like CHILLGUY — there is no subject. Answer "no subject".

Do not consider how much attention any post received. Numbers of views, likes or shares
are not shown to you, deliberately: attention is measured elsewhere and mixing it into
this judgement would make the same evidence count twice.
