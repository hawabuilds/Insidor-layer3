06. Search and You

Two screens that answer the only two questions a user brings back after their first session: does a coin already exist for this thing I just saw, and what happened to the things I was waiting on. Search is the anti-duplicate surface. /you is the return surface.

Everything upstream of here is push — the feed decides what you look at. Search is the one place the user drives. One box takes a contract address, a $TICKER, an @handle, or plain English, and it never asks the user to pick a mode: a small mono chip inside the field on the right lights up CONTRACT, TICKER, POSTER or stays dark for free text. That chip is the entire mode UI. When a query is genuinely ambiguous — a bare word like ferry is both a plausible ticker and a plausible search term — we run both and section both, rather than guessing and being wrong silently.

Results are sectioned, not interleaved, because three object types have three column grammars and a terminal user scans columns, not rows. Stories get views, velocity, age and Lead. Coins get price, mcap, liquidity and a match stamp. Posts get a platform badge and the raw text. Interleaving them would force one lowest-common-denominator column set and destroy the scan. Above the sections sits one lifted winner row with a cyan left edge and a stated reason — and it is suppressed whenever the top two candidates score within 8% of each other, so we never assert confidence the ranker doesn't have.

One query, three object types, three grammars. Notice the coin section carries a sparkline and the post section carries the raw text — the same search surfaces the post and the chart side by side, which is the whole differentiator against a screener that only knows tokens and a social app that only knows posts. Row three of Coins is the important one: a second $FERRY with zero liquidity still renders as a coin that exists, stamped UNVERIFIED, with Buy withheld and only View anyway offered. A zero-liquidity mint is not an absent mint, and treating the two as the same value is how a screen ends up telling a user "you'd be first" about the hottest ticker on the board.

The honest record

The Finished chip is the credibility surface, and it is deliberately uncomfortable. It opens on All — as a route constant, not a remembered preference — and it states its denominator in the first band before it shows a single row. The reason the default is All and never Hits is structural: any filter that defaults to wins turns a record into a marketing page, and the moment a user notices the default is doing that, every number on the screen becomes suspect, including the true ones. A product whose entire claim is "we saw it before crypto did" cannot afford a scoreboard that quietly hides the times it didn't. So misses sit in the same table, in the same row height, in the same type weight, and are never coloured red — red is a market colour here, reserved for loss and risk, and colouring a miss red would code an honest outcome as a failure state. A miss renders in --muted: present, legible, unremarkable.

Rows 3 and 7 are misses and row 6 is a case where we declined to name the coin at all. All three are the same height and the same weight as row 1 — no red, no strikethrough, no collapse. Lead is sortable ascending in one click, which is the fastest way for a sceptic to put our worst results on top; a record you cannot sort against yourself is a brochure. Proof is two external permalinks because the two timestamps that matter belong to X and to DexScreener, and a claim of earliness is worth nothing if the only witness is us.

/you — the return surface

The star has no wallet and no account behind it. It writes against a device id and gets claimed on first login, because the moment a user cares most about a story is the moment before they have any reason to trust us with a signature. /you stacks by urgency, and empty sections are unrendered rather than shown empty: Just minted (confirmed in the last 24h, unopened, pinned with a lime edge) · Waiting — no coin yet · With coins · Coins you watch · Gone quiet, collapsed with a one-tap Clear all 7. That last section exists because watchlist rot — twelve dead rows you scroll past every visit — is the actual mechanism by which watchlists get abandoned, and the fix is to make clearing them a single deliberate action rather than twelve.

A follow never silently converts. When a watched story mints, the row stays a story follow, because a story can mint again; adopting the coin requires an explicit + Watch $FERRY too, which writes a second row. The alternative — quietly repointing the follow at the token — means a user who starred a narrative wakes up watching a chart they never chose.

Urgency descends down the page and nothing is padded: sections with zero rows are not rendered at all, so a user with one starred story sees one section, not five empty headers. The position row is the only place a P/L number appears outside the coin page, and it carries BOUGHT 34m EARLY next to the ticker — the lead time is a property of the position, not of the marketing, and it is computed from the same promoted_at clock the record uses.

Why the notification is the hook, not the list

The watchlist alone does not bring anyone back. A user who has to remember to open an app to check whether something happened will stop opening the app, and the list will be right there, stale, confirming that the visit was wasted. The retention mechanism is the inverse: we open the app for them, at the one moment there is something to do. The mint notification fires on confirmed_coin_count: 0 → ≥1 and only on a confirmed match — an unsure match never pushes, because "your story just minted" on a 0.6-confidence guess is exactly the harm the abstain band exists to prevent. Line one is the story then the ticker — "Harbor Last Horn is now $FERRY" — in that order, because the user followed a story and has to recognise it before they can parse a symbol they have never seen. Line two carries the honest lead, and when the lead is negative it occupies the same slot at the same size: crypto traders got here 6m before us. Above the primary button, always, at the weight of the lead line: the fee, the price impact, and the tokens received. A push-driven buy is the highest-pressure buy in the product and it is the last place an undisclosed fee is acceptable.

Miss the toast and the next open leads with a while-you-were-away band, every number recomputed at render rather than replayed from the payload — "$4.1K now (was $18.4K)" — so a notification held through quiet hours can never sell yesterday's price as today's. That band is also the only place we ever ask for push permission: after we have already been useful once, never on first load.
