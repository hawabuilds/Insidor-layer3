01. Live Feed — the posts lane

A ranked board of the forty posts going viral right now, ordered by how fast each is accelerating against its own author's baseline — not by how many views it has. One job: what is blowing up, and how much head start is left.

This is deliberately the pre-coin surface. Most rows have no ticker and nothing to buy — not a gap in the data but the product's whole claim, since a row with a liquid coin attached means you were late. Inside Live feed a switch splits POSTS, STORIES and COINS, which share one row height, one header and one stability model, so the three read as views of a single instrument.

Below sits a 44px control strip: platform chips (rendered from select distinct platform, so X and TikTok and nothing else — three hardcoded chips reading zero forever would be a lie), age window, sort chips, thresholds, and one loud red SHOW RISKY. That toggle is the only control that relaxes a hard eligibility gate, which is why it is red, in the open rather than a drawer, and never persisted. On the right the strip reports on itself — LAST TICK 4s — because a board that reranks silently looks identical to one that has died.

Six of forty rows. The left edge carries a 3px lifecycle rail — cyan peaking, lime heating, dim cooling. Rows 1 and 2 are one story on two platforms; with COLLAPSE BY STORY off they render individually but share one resolved action, because twelve rows of a story is not twelve independent Buy buttons.

Why REACH is grey and HEAT is white

REACH is the one number here we refuse to render in primary ink. Cumulative views are an eligibility gate, not the score — a post clears a floor to be on the board at all, and after that its total says almost nothing about whether you still have a head start. Every competing surface trains users to read the biggest number in a row as the ranking. Render 2.4M in white beside a HEAT of 94 and the eye lands on the reach, re-sorting the board in the trader's head. Muting it costs nothing and kills the likeliest misread of this screen. HEAT takes the white number and the cyan bar because HEAT is the sort key made legible.

Why ACCEL shows an em-dash

ACCEL turns about forty seconds before the rank does, which is what a trader actually watches. It is a ratio of two exponentially-weighted view-rate accumulators, so it needs two snapshots to exist. On a post seen exactly once the honest value is not computable, and the obvious placeholder, 1.0×, is the worst available lie: it reads as steady state, sits in the amber band, and is indistinguishable from a real measurement of a post that is genuinely flat. So one snapshot renders a dim —, a blank sparkline and an asterisked HEAT. Same rule under an ingest gap: the accumulator freezes rather than decays, and the window is marked unmeasurable instead of scored as a win.

There is not a single like button on this screen

Nothing inside the POST cell is an engagement control. No like, no repost, no reply, no follow — and this is not an omission we plan to fill. Insidor is not a client for X or TikTok and must never look like one: the moment a row offers a repost affordance the board becomes a feed, the mental model becomes consumption, and every honesty label reads as decoration around content rather than a fact about an instrument. Name, handle and timestamp link out to the real post; that is where you engage. Create coin and Buy are Insidor actions on an Insidor object, so they sit outside the post cell behind a 1px divider separating what the internet made from what we are offering you.

The action is a pure function of one number: how many coins on this post's story are matched confirmed and clear the hard safety gates. Zero gives Create coin, one gives Buy $TICKER, two or more gives See the N coins. That count is market-data independent — a coin confirmed four minutes ago with a null mcap is still one, still Buy. Demanding a non-null mcap first would flip that mint back to Create and drive a duplicate launch in the exact window this product exists to serve.

One primary control per row. Study the third: when the matcher abstains the candidate is still shown — muted, dashed, labelled UNVERIFIED — but Buy is unrenderable, not disabled. Abstain never means hide, and never means Buy. Confidence stays categorical for the same reason: "73% match" beside an actionable button reads as good enough.

The rail

Above 1280px a 460px rail runs beside the board and logs what it just did. It exists because values update live but positions do not: ranks commit every 20 seconds behind hysteresis, so movement a user would otherwise miss has to be narrated somewhere. It is also where failures get published rather than buried.
