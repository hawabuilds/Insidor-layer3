11. The build plan

Eight stages, in order. Each one has to work before the next one makes any sense — and the first one is not setup. It is the question the whole company rests on, and it comes before we write a line of the product.

The temptation with a plan like this is to start with the visible things: the feed, the page, the buttons. We are deliberately not doing that. Everything visible in this deck sits on top of a single assumption — that our system genuinely finds memeable stories early — and that assumption has never been tested. Every setting in the collector, every cut-off, every filter was an educated guess made by one person in a week. So the first stage tests the guess.

Stage 0 · comes first · days, not months

Prove it actually works

The method is to work backwards. Instead of asking "will our system find good posts?" and waiting to see, we take coins that already did well over the last few days, trace each one back to the viral post it came from, and then check our own records: did we see that post, and how early? The answer already exists in the data we have been collecting. We just have to go and look.

Then the harder half. What did the winners have in common — the kind of story, the platform it started on, the shape of the growth, the language it was in? And critically, the comparison group: posts that went hugely viral and never became anything at all. If the winners and the nothings look identical to us, we have no signal. If they look different, that difference is the product.

STEP ONE
Start at the end
List the coins that actually made money in the last few days. Real outcomes, not our opinions.
STEP TWO
Trace each one home
Find the post that started it. Then check whether it is in our records, and what time we first wrote it down.
STEP THREE
Compare against the nothings
Do the same for posts that went viral and died. The difference between the two piles is the thing we are selling.

Here is what the working file looks like. This is not a product screen — it is our own worksheet, the thing we sit and read on the Monday morning after the week's data has been collected. Nothing on it is an opinion. It is coins, posts, view counts and clock times.

The comparison group — posts that went viral and became nothing

3.1M views · 5h
A politician's remark at a press conference
No object, no character, no name. Nothing to draw.
2.8M views · 8h
A last-minute goal in a league match
Enormous audience, but it belongs to a club and a sport, not to the internet.
1.9M views · 3h
A breaking news bulletin about a storm
Serious. Nobody wants a coin about it, and nobody would buy one.

All three are bigger than $FERRY was and none of them became anything. Size is not the signal. One absurd, nameable thing is — a frog called Gerald, a plastic plant, a ferry horn. Stage 0 is how we find out whether our system already knows that, or only we do.

Stage 0 does two more things while it is running. The code gets tidied and organised at the same time, so that stages 1 to 7 are built on a foundation somebody else could pick up. And every coin we trace, every post we match, every miss we write down becomes the first rows of the dataset behind Stage 7 — the record of which viral posts became coins that made money. That record is the thing a competitor cannot buy, and it starts being written here.

How the stages depend on each other

Read left to right and each stage needs the one before it — there is no point building trading into a page that does not exist. The line most people miss is the long one underneath: Stage 7 is not a late idea, it is the harvest of a crop planted in Stage 0. If we do not start recording on day one, Stage 7 is simply not available later at any price.

The one thing that has to be true

Every feature in this deck assumes our system genuinely finds memeable stories early.

The beautiful narrative page, the coin list, the one-tap create, the comments, the watchlist — all of it is worthless if what we surface is noise. No amount of design rescues a feed full of things nobody would ever make a coin out of. There is no supplier to call and no feature to add that fixes it.

That is why Stage 0 comes first, and why we should not build deep until it is answered. It can be answered in days, not months — the data is already sitting in our own records — and both possible answers are useful.

If the signal is real

We have proof, not a story — traced coins, timestamps, and a measured head start we can put in front of an investor or a first user.

If the signal is not real

We find out before wasting a month, and the misses tell us precisely what to change — more platforms, more languages, different filters.

Either outcome is worth having. The only bad version of this plan is the one where we build stages 1 through 7 first and find out at the end.
