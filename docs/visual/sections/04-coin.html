04. The Coin page — chart, swap, trade

One coin, told honestly, and the only surface in Insidor where money moves. Chart, stats and swap box are table stakes. The job only this page can do is prove this coin exists because of a story we caught first, say by how many minutes, and refuse to sell you anything on a claim we do not stand behind.

Above the fold, Axiom, Photon and DexScreener are the same page: candles, market cap, holders, a swap box, all of it fast. Copying that is table stakes and no reason for anyone to switch. The one row nobody else can render is the origin band — the post that started it, the clock time we promoted the story, the clock time crypto arrived, and the difference. It sits directly under the identity header, above the stats and the chart, drawn in the terminal's own ranked-row grammar rather than as a hero card. A hero card reads as a boast; a row with column headers and right-aligned mono reads as a measurement.

The band prints clock times, not relative ages: 13:59 and 14:33 are checkable against the permalinks, "34 minutes ago" is not. SPOTTED is our promote time, not the post's birth — the ferry post had been up for hours before it accelerated, and the claim is earliness versus crypto, never versus the internet. The big +34m is Insidor's frozen record. The small your lead now +18m beneath it is a different number — where the reader stands right now, counting down, flipping amber to crypto caught up 7m ago and red past thirty minutes. They never share a component, because conflating the detector's history with the reader's live standing is the most flattering lie this product could tell.

The fee line, and why it is boring on purpose

All five recap rows render at one uniform 12px mono weight and all five come from the order response — nothing is a literal in the client. The fee row gets no badge, no box, no colour, no acknowledgement checkbox: a highlighted disclosure is something users learn to dismiss, whereas a row sitting in the same column as the number they care about is something they read. The ⓘ opens the split, and the split states the constraint plainly rather than pretending 0.50% was a price we chose.

Two things to hold onto. The floor is real: the cheapest possible cost to a user is 0.50% per trade, because Jupiter enforces a 50 bps minimum on integrator fees, with our platform share on top. We cannot undercut it, so we disclose it. And the number moves — Jupiter's platform fee is published at 50 bps and measured live at 10; if they flip it back our disclosed total doubles to 1.00% overnight. Live value versus a stable "up to 1.0%" is open question #6, a trust decision rather than an engineering one. Either way the displayed fee is frozen from the order that produced the signed transaction and re-asserted server-side against that requestId in /api/execute. The client never computes it.

We do not block on unknown. Blocking every buy whose safety checks have not returned yet would delete the product, because the buys worth making are all 40 seconds old. Unknown discloses; only four things block, and only in the buy direction.

Safety signals that actually discriminate

Every competitor's safety panel gives every pump.fun token a wall of green ticks, because mint and freeze authority are revoked by construction on all of them. A tick every token earns teaches users that green means safe, inverting the check. So those two collapse into one dim non-signal line and green is reserved for checks a token can fail. Four things block a buy: active mint authority, active freeze authority, a Token-2022 transfer fee or hook, and no sell route — a reverse order call for ~1% of the intended out. Buy route present with sell route absent is the honeypot signature and is worth more than the other three combined. Selling is never blocked in any state, because stranding a holder just relocates the unfairness; a transfer-fee token discloses in amber and the fee is subtracted from You receive as a sixth recap row.

The third card is the most important state in the deck. On an unverified match the panel still quotes and Sell still works, but Buy does not exist. A greyed button is an offer withheld; an absent button is a claim we never made. The buy flow, state by state

Quotes debounce at 250ms into POST /api/order, with the referral account and fee injected server-side from env and never accepted from the client. TTL is 20s, refreshed every 15s while visible, and suspended entirely while the confirm dialog is open. A refresh moving outAmount more than 1.0% against the user flashes amber and holds. requestId is single-use, and the trades row is written at ORDERING with request_id unique — the database, not the UI, enforces single submission across tabs.

  1. no walletPage is fully readable and still quoting. Position card is absent, button reads Connect wallet to buy. Amount and settings survive the Privy round trip.
  2. insufficient balanceNot disabled. Cyan Add 0.34 SOL, opening the funding sheet with the shortfall prefilled. A disabled button here is a dead end; a shortfall is a next step.
  3. back from fundingIf price drifted more than 5%: "When you left, $89.20 bought 110,420 FERRY. It now buys 46,110 (−58%)." at equal weight, with Buy anyway / Change amount. Intents older than 30 minutes, or for another mint, are discarded.
  4. quotingHolds in QUOTING until the mint account answers with decimals. A receive figure wrong by a power of ten is worse than a spinner.
  5. quote expiry20s TTL, refresh at 15s. Adverse move >1.0% flashes amber and waits for an explicit tap.
  6. signingConfirm dialog repeats the uncollapsed fee split. Declining reads You cancelled. Nothing was sent. in --muted, never red — declining is a normal outcome, not an error.
  7. submitted/api/execute re-asserts the frozen fee against the requestId and captures SOL/USD server-side into sol_usd_at_trade. Every PnL number on this page derives from that column, never from the browser's SOL price global.
  8. landingGET /api/txstatus?sig= polls at 1s for 15s then 3s. A fill is confirmed by signature status, never inferred from a balance change. Past 15s: Still confirming (0:47), polling to 90s.
  9. filledFilled — 110,420 $FERRY, sub-line 0.50 SOL · avg $0.00080783, signature copyable, position card flashing in behind.
  10. failed, sig exists"Price moved more than your 1% limit. Nothing was spent except the network fee." — with the signature and a Solscan link, plus Retry at 3% pre-computed. Expired blockhash reads did not land in time and retry bumps priority one tier.
  11. failed, no sig"We lost contact before confirming. Check your balance before retrying." + Check status. Never auto-retry. That is how a user gets filled twice.

Slippage defaults to Auto (Jupiter's RTSE) for anything under thirty minutes old. A fixed 1% on a live bonding curve reverts constantly, and every revert is a trust event even though nothing was lost but the network fee.

The funding cliff, stated honestly

For a user arriving from a TikTok link with no SOL, the path to holding $FERRY is not one flow but two: card → SOL through the onramp, then SOL → FERRY through the swap. That is roughly 2.5% on the onramp leg plus 0.60% on the swap, and it takes long enough that the price on the second leg is not the price they saw on the first. This is the single largest drop-off in the product and we should not pretend otherwise. Direct-to-mint fiat funding is cut from v1 because it is unverified that the onramp can deliver an arbitrary SPL mint, and shipping on that assumption makes the normie path fiction. We ship honest arithmetic instead: both legs and both fees stated end to end before the user starts, the shortfall prefilled, and the drift on return shown at equal weight behind an explicit Buy anyway.
