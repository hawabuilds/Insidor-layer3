02. Live Feed — the coins lane

The same board, switched to the tradeable side: what is moving right now, and — in the slot every competitor fills with a decorative sparkline — where it came from. One job: turn a story into the specific mint you can buy, without ever showing a number we do not have.

The posts lane answers what is blowing up; the coins lane answers what can I do about it. Same instrument — same 54px rows, hairlines, rank column, 20-second commit. The one real difference is the STORY column, and that column is the entire company. DexScreener, Photon, Axiom and Pump.fun render the same eleven market columns off the same three vendors; none can tell you this mint is the ferry captain's last horn, that we saw the post 34 minutes before crypto Twitter did, and that the trace is auditable at a URL. So it sits in slot three, right after the ticker, and it is the last column the responsive rules may drop.

The board is never one list. Four bands — Fresh (on the bonding curve), Graduating, Live (migrated, has a pool), From a Story (one row per narrative) — each with its own column template and sort key. A three-minute $8K curve token and a $40M AMM token are not comparable quantities, and a table showing both draws four structural dashes per row across a whole band: a table admitting it was built for the wrong data.

How a 31-minute-old token ranks against a three-day-old one

Four mechanisms, none a hand-tuned weight. Band partition: a curve token lives in Fresh and cannot enter the same ordered list as a migrated one. Market cap and liquidity are never terms — display columns and hard gates only, which is why $MOTH's fifteen-fold size advantage buys it nothing. Acceleration is self-normalised: five-minute volume against the coin's own last hour, so a coin is only compared to itself and being big earns zero. And in Fresh an age penalty (1.87× at twenty minutes, 7.94× at three hours) turns the band over completely in about forty minutes. One more is the thesis in the formula: a story-linked coin is multiplied by 0.35 the moment crypto Twitter picks the story up — when our edge is gone the coin falls off the board while it is still climbing.

Note what story attachment does not do: it never multiplies rank in the coin-subject bands, only filters and breaks ties. If it multiplied, the incentive would be to game the matcher — the precision-critical component in the system — so keeping story off the multiplier keeps the attack surface off the correctness-critical path.

The lookup returns null for absent numerics rather than coercing to zero. The consequence that keeps it honest: a missing mint timestamp renders as unknown age and suppresses Buy, instead of returning zero and painting a token of unknown vintage as brand new on a terminal that sells earliness.

The RISK chip: why there are no green ticks

Every competitor's safety panel opens with two permanent green ticks — mint authority revoked, freeze authority revoked. The pump.fun program revokes both at creation, so every token passes. A check the whole population passes carries zero bits, and worse, it teaches the user that green means safe in a panel where green means nothing; after a week of two ticks on every rug they stop reading it. Neither is rendered as a signal; both appear once in the popover footer as a dim non-signal line saying why.

What replaces them: worst discriminating signal on the left, fixed obtainability denominator on the right — T10 78 · 4/9. Nine signals would discriminate (dev holding, sniper, bundle and insider percentages, creator history, top-10 concentration, single-holder maximum, holder count, sell-side liveness); we can compute four today, and five need first-slot transaction analysis we are not building yet. The denominator is always visible because a missing chip reads as "fine", and a lime tick is unreachable until creator history has a verdict for that wallet — which is why $PIGEON reads ? 4/9.

Left: the popover names all nine signals, including the five we cannot compute — naming them as unavailable is the point, because omitting them would let four-of-four read as a clean bill of health. Right: the chip row we removed, and what replaced it.

Hard gates hide a row unless Show risky is on: live mint or freeze authority, Live liquidity under $3,000, the honeypot proxy, unknown mint time, and top-10 concentration above 85% on Fresh or 60% on Live. Band-dependent on purpose — a flat 60% gate empties Fresh on day one, because a three-minute-old token has nine buyers and is legitimately concentrated. The hidden count is always stated, since a gate that silently shrinks the board is indistinguishable from a broken query. And authority fields are tri-state: absent is unknown, not revoked, or one vendor timeout passes every row.

Finally, the action column. The primary is a cyan Buy, but it is navigation: it opens the coin page with the tradebox focused, where the quote is fresh and the fee is disclosed before confirm. The dim +0.5% fee line is read from config and is the cheapest number that can ever appear — the Jupiter integrator fee has a fifty-basis-point floor, and implying zero is a lie we would walk back at confirm. On an UNSURE row no Buy control renders at all, at any width, in any state. Abstain never means hide, and it never means Buy either.
