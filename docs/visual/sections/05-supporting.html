05. Trading, watchlist, search and trending

Four supporting features. None of them is the reason someone opens Insidor, but without them the person who spots a story early has to leave to act on it, forgets to come back, accidentally makes a coin that already exists, or can only see what we choose to push at them.

Trading — buying built into the page

When a narrative already has a coin, the buy box sits on the same page as the story. No copying a contract address into another app, no second tab, no losing the thing you were looking at. You read the two-liner, you look at the numbers, you buy. We take a small fee on every trade that goes through us, and that fee is shown in the order before you confirm — nothing hidden in the price.

We do not build the trading engine. Actually executing a trade on-chain — routing it, getting a good price, handling failures, not losing people's money — is hard, risky, and already solved by companies who do only that. The plan is to route orders through Axiom or Photon, pending confirmation that they permit it: research found neither publishes a public developer API, so the integration is not yet proven. The confirmed fallback is Jupiter, which is openly documented and pays us an integrator fee, where Photon charges the user 1% and pays us nothing.

Watchlist — the notification is the feature

Saving a narrative is easy to build and easy to ignore. The part that matters is what happens after: if a narrative you saved gets its first coin, we tell you within seconds. That is the whole retention mechanic. Instead of hoping the user remembers to check back, we open the app for them at the one moment there is actually something to do — a story they already believed in has just become tradeable, and almost nobody else knows yet.

The alert only fires on events worth waking someone for: a first coin on a saved narrative, or a new post joining a saved narrative. Not price moves, not daily digests. If we send noise, the user turns notifications off and the mechanic dies.

The notification carries the two-liner, not a ticker on its own — the user saved a story, so remind them which story. Tapping it lands on the coin with a buy button already there, two taps from lock screen to trade.

Search — one box, and it stops duplicates

One field takes three kinds of input: a keyword (“ferry”), a ticker ($FERRY), or a coin address pasted from anywhere. It returns both sides at once — the posts and narratives we are tracking, and the coins that exist. Its most useful job is unglamorous: a trader sees something funny on X, wants to make a coin out of it, searches, and finds three already exist. That one screen stops a lot of wasted money.

Trending — letting people browse

The feed shows what we think is worth seeing right now. Trending lets someone ignore that and look for themselves: the top narratives and the top coins, sorted by views or by age, newest first or biggest first. It is the release valve for anyone who does not want to be led — and the sort controls are the user's, not ours.

Two lists, two sort controls each, nothing else. The ordering follows the column the user picked — views or age on the left, market cap or age on the right — so the ranking is something they can check against reality rather than something they have to trust us about.

All four features lean on things we rent rather than build: Privy holds the wallet, Axiom or Photon (or Jupiter) executes the trade, DexScreener supplies price and liquidity, and the phone's own notification system delivers the alert. What is ours is the moment each one fires — knowing that a saved story just got its first coin, and that this is worth interrupting someone for.
