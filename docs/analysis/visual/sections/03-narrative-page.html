03. The narrative page

The screen the whole product is built around. One story, one page: what happened, how big it is now, which coins were made from it, where to buy, and what other traders think.

Every narrative gets its own web address — insidor.app/n/harbor-last-horn. A trader pastes that link into a group chat, and someone who has never heard of us opens it, understands the story in one sentence, and can act. Nothing on it needs an account to read. The page is the marketing.

It answers four questions in one place: what is this · is it still hot · which coin is the real one · what do other traders think. Today those answers live in four separate tabs — a video app, a chart, a token scanner, a group chat. Putting them on one page is most of the product.

The story, and how viral it is

The top of the page is the clip and the plain-English two-liner. That sentence is the most important text in the product — it is what a stranger reads first. Beside it sit facts only: views, reposts, likes, age, and the view counts we recorded ourselves as the story grew. Nobody sells "what did this post look like twenty minutes ago", so we write it down as it happens and show it back plainly.

Why no button on $BOAT. We are not sure this coin belongs to this story. It might; it might be riding a different one. We would rather show it and say so than hide it, and rather leave the button off than help you buy something we cannot stand behind. You can still open it on any explorer.

Every number here is a fact about the world — views, reposts, likes, market cap, liquidity, holders, times. Our judgement shows up in what we chose to put on the page, never in a number printed next to it.

Why every coin is listed, not just one

When something goes viral, several people make a coin within minutes. One is the real one the crowd settles on; the rest are copycats riding the attention. Showing only "the coin" would mean a user sometimes buys a copycat and loses money because of a silent decision of ours. So we list all of them, labelled: First — made from this story before any other. Biggest — the most money in it now. Those are usually different coins, and which one you want depends on how you trade.

The third label matters most. Unverified means we think a coin relates to this story but we are not confident, and it gets no buy button at all — not a greyed-out one. In the founder's words: "A greyed-out button looks like something we're holding back; no button at all is us not making a claim we can't stand behind."

Trading and research, on the same page

Buying happens here, not in another app — and we rent the exchange rather than build it. The plan is to route orders through Axiom or Photon, pending confirmation they permit it; neither publishes a public developer interface today, so the confirmed fallback is Jupiter, which works now and pays us a fee for the business. Wallets are handled by Privy; charts, safety checks and holder data by DexScreener, RugCheck and Helius. All plumbing. What we build is the part above — finding the story before the coin existed.

The thesis layer

At the bottom, traders write what they think. Two facts attach automatically at the moment of posting and can never be edited: how big the story was when they said it, and whether they own the coin — holding, sold, or no position, read from the connected wallet. Someone who called this at 340,000 views reads very differently from someone who turned up at 2.4 million; someone talking up a coin they hold reads differently from someone with nothing on the table. Neither is disqualifying. Both are things you would want to know.

The two pills beside each name are the whole point: the story's size and the wallet's holdings, both frozen at the moment the take was posted.

Why nobody else can build this layer

It looks like a comment section. It is not, because of who can attach those two facts. Twitter knows what people say but not what they own. A trading app knows what they own but not what they think. Insidor is the only place the take and the wallet arrive together, on the same story, at the same moment — so the record writes itself and compounds. That history is why traders stay: their calls and their reputation live here.

The same argument as the view-count history: the only way to have it is to have been recording since day one.

What is deliberately not on this page

None of our own scoring. No heat number, no acceleration figure, no confidence percentage on the unverified coin. The page carries facts about the world and our decisions — which story to show, which coins to list, which one gets a button. The user gets the judgement, not the arithmetic.
