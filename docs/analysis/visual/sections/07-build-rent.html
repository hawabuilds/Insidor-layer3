07. What we build, what we rent

One sentence carries this whole section: we build the detection, we rent everything else. Almost every moving part of Insidor already exists as a service somebody else runs better than we would. We pay for those and spend all of our own effort on the one thing nobody sells.

A small team's only real asset is where it points its attention. Every week spent writing a wallet system, a price chart or a trading engine is a week not spent on the reason the product exists. All of those things are solved problems with mature providers behind them; the thing we are trying to do — see a story going viral before a coin is made out of it — is not sold by anyone, because nobody else is recording it.

So the rule for every job in the system is the same question: can we buy this? If yes, we buy it and move on. If no, that is where the company lives.

Why the rented parts are not a risk

The usual worry about renting is dependence — what happens when a supplier raises its price or shuts the door? The answer is that every rented job here has at least two credible providers, and swapping one is a few days of work because none of them touch the part that makes us valuable. Our record of viral posts stays ours whichever wallet or chart provider is plugged in.

If the data reseller goes away

Several competitors sell the same feed. We change one supplier and keep collecting. Time to swap: ~2 days

If a trading venue says no

Jupiter is already confirmed and working. The trade button changes where it points; nothing else moves. Time to swap: ~3 days

If our own detection is wrong

There is no supplier to call. This is the one failure we cannot buy our way out of — which is exactly why Stage 0 tests it first. Unrecoverable

The only genuinely dangerous box on the page is the one we own. Everything rented has a second option behind it.

An honest note on trading

Stated plainly, because this one is not settled

The plan is to route trades through Axiom or Photon, pending confirmation that they permit it — and that confirmation has not been given. Research found neither publishes a public developer interface: Axiom's own documentation index has zero developer entries, and QuickNode's directory states outright that Photon does not support integrations of this kind.

The working alternative is Jupiter, which is confirmed, open, and pays us an integrator fee — where Photon charges the user 1% and pays us nothing. So Axiom or Photon is the plan; Jupiter is the fallback that already works, and the product is designed so that either can sit behind the same button.

The point of this section is the shape, not the supplier list. We are building one thing and paying other people to do six. If a reviewer wants to know what Insidor actually is, it is the cyan box: finding the viral post before the coin exists, and grouping it into a narrative. That is the company. Everything else is plumbing we rent.
