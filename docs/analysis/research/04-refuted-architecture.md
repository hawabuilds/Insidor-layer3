**Holds:** False
**Correction:** Reorder the tests and change the variance model.

1. Run the data-availability audit FIRST, before any residual work — it is one query and it may kill the design. Join post_snapshots to narrative_posts.posted_at and compute the distribution of (first captured_at − posted_at). Given MIN_INGEST_VIEWS=30_000 (worker/ingest.js:60), INGEST_MS=10 min (worker/lib/pipeline-intervals.js), and cold-tier 20-min spacing for single-snapshot posts (worker/snapshotter.js:56-58), the near-certain result is p50 first-observation age well above 9 min with the 4–30 min bin both sparse and selected on "hit 30k views fast." If so, z_a at τ≈9 min is not implementable without lowering the ingest view gate and tightening young-post cadence — an API-spend and architecture change, not a storage change. Decide that before fitting anything.

2. Drop the per-age-bucket σ table. The spread at fixed τ is driven by the expected count, not the age: σ_log ≈ 1/√μ with μ = exp(β_a)·τ^γ, so σ varies ~10–30× across authors at a single τ. Use a mean-variance relation instead of a lookup — fit a negative-binomial (or quasi-Poisson) GLM with log link, offset γ·log τ, and per-author intercept, and derive σ from the fitted μ and dispersion. Same storage as today, no table, and it handles zero and single-digit counts natively instead of breaking on them. If you want a transform-based shortcut, Anscombe (2√(y + 3/8)) stabilizes variance without the log1p bias that systematically inflates z_a for low-baseline authors.

3. Make the denominator honest about estimation error: σ̂_a² = σ²_within(μ) + Var(β̂_a), and shrink β̂_a toward a follower-count-bucket prior so new and thin-history authors degrade to the prior rather than producing undefined or explosive z. This is also the only thing that gives any coverage of fresh burner accounts.

4. Calibrate the trigger on empirical residual quantiles, not Gaussian z. Pick the cut from the observed upper tail at a target alert budget; do not assume z=3 means 1.3/1000.

5. Treat γ as at minimum two-regime (pre- and post-amplification) or add a posting-hour term. A single global γ leaves clock-hour effects in the residual, which a variance fix cannot remove.

6. Ship z_a as a re-ranker on top of the existing absolute views-velocity gate, never as a standalone trigger. It is author-controlled and therefore cheap to game; absolute velocity to 30k views is not.
FACTUAL — the citation does not support the extension, and the named failure mode is the source's default, not a novel risk.

Szabó & Huberman (arXiv:0811.0405) fit ln N_s(t2) = ln r(t1,t2) + ln N_s(t1) + ξ_s(t1,t2). Three mismatches:
(a) The random effect ξ_s is indexed per SUBMISSION s, not per submitter. The paper never estimates a per-author level. More importantly the model CONDITIONS on the observed early count N_s(t1) — the level enters as a measured quantity with zero estimation error. Replacing it with a β̂_a fitted from author history is not a reparameterization, it is a different estimator whose denominator must be sqrt(σ²_within + Var(β̂_a) + between-post variance within author). The claim's σ̂_a silently absorbs three variance components S&H never had to separate. For an author with a short history, Var(β̂_a) dominates and z_a is mostly measuring how little you know about that author.
(b) The paper's shortest indicator times are 1 digg-hour and 7 days for YouTube — 6.7× and 1120× longer than τ=9 min. The claim is honest that it did not find literature verification at this timescale, but then treats the gap as a modest extrapolation. It is not; it crosses from the large-count regime into the small-count regime (see below), where the log-linear form changes character.
(c) The homoskedasticity S&H verified is with respect to POPULARITY LEVEL at a fixed (t1,t2) pair — "we checked the means and variances of the residuals as a function of N_c(t_i)... both the mean and variance are independent of N_c(t_i)." σ₀² = var(r_c) is re-estimated for every (t1,t2) fit. So σ being time-indexed is built into the source model by construction. The claim proposes to spend the first two weeks of work testing a hypothesis the primary source already answers in the affirmative.

The axis is also wrong. At τ≈9 min the residual spread is not primarily a function of τ — it is a function of the expected count. Under Poisson/NB noise σ_log ≈ 1/√μ, and μ = exp(β_a)·τ^γ. Across authors at a fixed τ=9 min, μ spans two to three orders of magnitude, so σ varies roughly 10–30× at that single τ. A σ table indexed by τ alone cannot flatten that. You would build the table, find over/under-triggering persists across authors rather than across ages, and rebuild. That is the implementation time this review is meant to save.

Two more mis-specifications the claim does not mention, both larger than τ-heteroskedasticity:
- Zeros and small integers. At 9 minutes, weighted engagement (worker/lib/velocity.js: likes + 2·reposts + replies) is 0–3 for most non-top-tier authors. log is undefined at 0; log1p biases β̂_a downward in a level-dependent way, systematically inflating z_a for low-baseline accounts. That is a structural false-positive generator aimed precisely at small accounts.
- Tail calibration. A z=3 cut implies ~1.3 false positives per 1000 only under Gaussian residuals. Early-window log residuals on X are right-skewed and fat-tailed (quote-tweet cascades, purchased engagement bursts). Mild excess kurtosis puts the true rate 5–20× higher. Fixing σ does not fix a mis-specified μ either: a global γ averages over two distinct regimes — organic follower-graph decay and a discrete For-You amplification step — so residuals partly encode "posted at an unusual hour" rather than "unusual engagement."

ENGINEERING — τ≈9 min is not observable in this pipeline, so the proposed test will return a confidently wrong answer.

From the code: /Users/zainkhaliq/Desktop/Claude_Cowork/Insidor/worker/ingest.js:60 sets MIN_INGEST_VIEWS = 30_000 — a post does not enter narrative_posts at all until it already has 30k views. /Users/zainkhaliq/Desktop/Claude_Cowork/Insidor/worker/lib/pipeline-intervals.js sets INGEST_MS = 10 min, so admission is quantized to 10-minute cycles on top of search-API indexing lag. /Users/zainkhaliq/Desktop/Claude_Cowork/Insidor/worker/snapshotter.js:56-58 sets HOT/WARM/COLD intervals of 2/8/20 min, and assignTiers ranks by viewsVelocity, which returns null for a post with fewer than two snapshots — so a newly ingested post sorts to the bottom and lands in COLD, meaning its second snapshot arrives ~20 minutes after its first.

Consequences: (1) the 4–30 min window is the sparsest region of post_snapshots, not a well-populated one; (2) the rows that do exist there are the posts that cleared 30k views within minutes — the sample is selected on the outcome z_a is supposed to detect. Binning those residuals by τ and finding the spread "flat" would be an artifact of conditioning on success. The proposed test cannot fail informatively.

The claimed fix is also mis-scoped. Making τ≈9 min observable requires lowering or removing the 30k view gate and tightening ingest cadence for young posts — that is materially more API spend against the same daily budget the pipeline already throttles hard (worker/score.js runs SCORE_TOP_N=10 per 60s cycle under an Anthropic daily cap with an explicit dormancy path). That is an architecture and cost change, not "more storage, same architecture."

Cold start is worse than the claim allows. β̂_a is undefined for a brand-new author, and fresh burner accounts are exactly the population a memecoin insider terminal exists to catch — so the model has zero coverage on its highest-value segment. The "first seen when already large" case is already a live path in the codebase (qualifiesFastLane in worker/score.js: views ≥ 500k, age ≤ 120 min) and by construction has no τ=9 min observation, so z_a cannot serve it.

PRODUCT — an author-relative z-score on public engagement is a manipulation surface, and the cost of a false positive is asymmetric.

β̂_a is under the adversary's control in both directions: an operator can depress their own baseline with low-engagement filler posts, or buy ~100 likes for a few dollars on the target post. Either moves z_a further than a genuine organic signal does. The whole premise of the product is that the reader acts early and with money; a detector that is cheapest to trigger for the party who benefits from triggering it inverts the product's value. Absolute views velocity — already implemented and already the ranking signal — is harder to fake to a 30k threshold than an author-relative deviation is to fake to z=3. Adding z_a as a trigger rather than a tiebreaker strictly increases attack surface.

Directionally the claim is right that a per-author baseline is more informative than a global one, and right that residual spread is not constant. It is wrong about the citation, wrong about which axis the spread varies on, wrong that the fix is storage-only, and wrong about test ordering — which together is several weeks of misdirected work.

FACTUAL — the numbers survive; the inference from them does not. 30-42k mints/day is defensible for July 2026 (Cryptobriefing, 2026-06-10, cites ~42,000 Solana tokens in 24h; The Block and Dune-derived reporting put early-Jan-2026 pump.fun launches near 25-30k/day at ~70% share). Arithmetic checks: 30000/48=625, 42000/48=875. So "600-900 in 30 minutes" is right. But that is the MARGINAL count, and the design never uses time marginally — it uses it conjunctively with a ticker string extracted from the post. The correct quantity is same-ticker mint density, and I measured it against the very API the code calls: $TRUMP = 7 distinct Solana mints spread over 563 days; $PUMP = 4 mints over 70 days; $MOON = 6 over 291 days; $KANG = 1. Conditional on an exact symbol match, competing candidates are separated by MONTHS, so a 30-minute window collapses the set to essentially one. Time is not near-zero evidence; it is the most discriminative feature available. Even taken marginally, 48x reduction is ~5.6 bits, which is not "near-zero." The stated conditional is also the wrong sensitivity: what would degrade the gate is a spike in same-ticker minting (copycat swarms on a hot narrative), not a change in total mint volume — total volume could triple with no effect.

ENGINEERING — the pairCreatedAt concern is directionally right but wrong on mechanism, wrong on magnitude, and wrong about the remedy, and the wrongness runs in OPPOSITE directions in the two regimes. I tested both live. (1) Brand-new bonding-curve token FK41oMGymJVrZMhGNTmgkguasrYtbDhCGNWnQwkzpump: DexScreener pumpfun pairCreatedAt = 2026-07-27T01:26:19Z; I paginated getSignaturesForAddress across 12 pages (11,476 sigs) to the genesis tx = 2026-07-27T01:26:19Z. EXACT to the second, not "hours." For the early regime the product exists to serve, pairCreatedAt IS on-chain mint time. (2) Mature token Fartcoin (9BB6NFEcjBCtnNLFko2FqVQBq8HHM13kCyYcdQbgpump): lookupMint() returns ageMin=602301 (418.3 days) against a true mint age of 646.8 days — a 228-DAY error, not hours. The cause is not that pairCreatedAt means pool creation; it is that comparePairs() ranks SOL-quote then vol24h, so for a graduated token it selects whichever pool later became most liquid (here an Orca pool created 2025-06-03, ~8 months post-mint) and the original pumpfun bonding-curve pair has been dropped from the response entirely. The claim's implied remedy — build a true on-chain mint-time oracle — is the expensive wrong turn: my RPC pagination cost 12 calls for a 40-minute-old token and scales with signature count, so it would cost hundreds of calls per mature token, on a latency-critical path.

PRODUCT — for a product whose value is being early and where a false positive costs money, the claim inverts the risk. The live bug already recorded in docs/DESIGN.md line 83 ("gym day" and an unrelated post both matching ticker PUMP to the real $1.84B token, flagged canonical/first_deployed) is EXACTLY the failure a temporal gate prevents: real PUMP minted 2025-07-10, the posts are 2026. Current code has no temporal gate and picks by volume, which is precisely why it locks onto the largest token. De-weighting time would entrench the false positive the design is meant to kill. Separately, lib/token-lookup.js:70 is fail-dangerous: `if (!pair.pairCreatedAt) return 0;` renders missing timestamp as ageMin=0, i.e. "brand new" — maximally attractive on a terminal that sells earliness. Missing data must fail closed (null/unknown, suppress the buy affordance), never as zero. Also note lines 153/171 return found:false when liquidity<=0, so a just-minted token with no pool yet is invisible — a real earliness gap, but one caused by the liquidity floor, not by pairCreatedAt.
